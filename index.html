About

A model that segments a tumor is only useful in a clinic if someone knows when to distrust it. That question runs through everything I do: segmentation architectures that stay small enough to deploy, uncertainty estimates that flag the voxels a radiologist should look at again, and domain adaptation for the patients who look nothing like the training set.

My dissertation extends this into physics-informed territory — coupling PDE-based tumor growth models with Bayesian inference, and asking which growth dynamics are actually recoverable from imaging that is sparse and noisy.

Alongside the research I have spent seven years teaching circuits, signals, and senior design at ODU, and mentored two NSF-REU students on privacy-preserving medical image analysis.

What I study

Research Focus

Efficient Tumor Segmentation

"Can a segmentation network shrink enough to run in a hospital without losing accuracy?"

UNCE-NODE replaces the discrete residual stack of a U-Net with a neural ordinary differential equation, sharing weights across integration steps. The result is roughly 30% fewer trainable parameters at comparable Dice accuracy. Related work extends the same encoder to Sub-Saharan African glioma cohorts and to inpainting healthy tissue for BraTS 2023.

Recurrence vs. Radiation Necrosis

"After treatment, does this scan show the tumor returning or the tissue reacting?"

The two look nearly identical on MRI and call for opposite clinical decisions. I extract multiresolution radiomic and fractal texture features to separate them, and pair that with a copula-based survival model that handles dependent censoring — a case standard Cox regression quietly assumes away.

Knowing When a Model Is Uncertain

"Which of these segmentations should a human look at again?"

An ensemble of nnU-Nets trained with 5-fold cross-validation yields two complementary signals: entropy-based total uncertainty and mutual-information epistemic uncertainty. Together they stratify erroneous segmentations well enough to route difficult cases to a clinician, and class activation mapping shows which structures drove each decision. Our team placed eighth in the AMOS challenge at MICCAI 2022.

Functional Brain Networks

"Which functional networks actually carry the signal in opioid use disorder?"

Moving from structure to function: discrete wavelet features from resting-state fMRI BOLD signals, LASSO selection, and random forest classification separate opioid use disorder from healthy controls — and, more usefully, name the networks doing the discriminating. Interpretability was the design constraint here, not an afterthought.

Adult-to-Pediatric Domain Shift

"What survives when the target population is scarce and anatomically different?"

Pediatric glioma datasets are orders of magnitude smaller than adult ones, and the tumors differ in location, anatomy, and enhancement. MF-DNN combines multiresolution fractal descriptors at the encoder bottleneck with a gradient-reversal module that pushes the network toward features an adult-versus-pediatric classifier cannot separate, plus Monte-Carlo dropout to flag low-confidence voxels for radiologist review.

Physics-Informed Growth Under Noise

"Which tumor growth dynamics can imaging this sparse actually resolve?"

Dissertation work in progress. PDE-based tumor growth models are constrained less by their physics than by how sparsely and noisily we sample the patient. I am developing a Resolvable Expressive Capacity framework with eigentask decomposition and a Bayesian inference layer, aimed at digital twins of tumor evolution that stay honest about what the available imaging can and cannot resolve.
